Parkinsons Disease (PD) is a complicated neurological illness that afflicts population worldwide. Envisaging how this disease will progress is very important to plan better and more effective treatments. One of the common tools used by doctors to check out PD symptoms is the Unified Parkinson’s Disease Rating Scale (UPDRS), which looks at both movement-related (motor) and other symptoms (non-motor). This project explores the use of voice recordings as a simple, diagnostic way to predict total UPDRS scores. Voice inputs can help in detecting early signs of the disease without the need for physical tests, making it easier for regular monitoring at home. To improve prediction, feature engineering and data augmentation techniques are used. To check how well the models work, two validation approaches are applied: splitting the dataset into 70/30 for training and testing respectively. During the classification phase, several machine learning models were tested, including Random Forest, Naive Bayes, XGBoost, K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN), and Artificial Neural Networks (ANN). In 70/30 split method, XGBoost models showed very high accuracy—up to 99.23%—while others were lower, around 92.25%, 89.53%, 81.1% and 41.27%, depending on the dataset. This method also helped reduce prediction errors. However, there were noticeable differences in accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score depending on the model and the validation method used. This suggests that the model’s performance can change based on how the data is split and tested. Even with these variations, the results highlight the strong potential of using voice-based tools to monitor PD. Such an approach can allow for remote, regular check-ups and improve how the disease is managed by detecting early symptoms.
